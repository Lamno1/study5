## What the literature establishes (foundational)

- **Stiglitz & Weiss (1981)** — asymmetric information → credit rationing; better borrower
  information can expand credit at the margin.
- **Pagano & Jappelli (1993)** — credit-bureau/registry information sharing expands lending; a new
  information source is worth *more* where existing sharing is *weaker* (the logic H-000500 rests on).
- **Berger & Udell (2006)** — lenders pick among lending technologies by information environment;
  whether a payment trace is usable depends on the lender's technology (supports E4).
- **Demirgüç-Kunt, Klapper, Singer & Ansar (2022)** / **Klapper, Singer, Starita & Norris (2025)**
  — the Findex framework; digital-payment receivers use other formal services more. Descriptive.

## Unresolved in the literature

1. **Substitute vs complement** at the individual level is unsettled — micro evidence and one
   market study say *complement*; firm and one market study say *substitute*. May be level- and
   channel-dependent. Study 5's negative interaction sits on the *complement / account-access* side.
2. Whether Galilea et al.'s firm-level coverage moderation extends to individual retail borrowers —
   **untested pre–Study 5.**
3. **The moderator itself.** The World Bank discontinued Doing Business on 16 Sept 2021 after the
   WilmerHale investigation found data irregularities in DB 2016–2020; the **Getting Credit**
   indicator specifically was manipulated for **China (DB2018)** and **Saudi Arabia (DB2020)** —
   **both are in the Study 5 sample.** This is directly material to SELFREVIEW concern C6.

## Candidate gaps

- **G1 (NOVELTY_CANDIDATE):** no cross-country individual-level test of the digital-payment ×
  credit-information-coverage interaction on formal borrowing. Left open by Galilea et al. (firms),
  Alok et al. (one country), Berg et al. (one lender), the Findex reports (descriptive).
- **G2 (NOVELTY_CANDIDATE):** the substitute-vs-complement question at the individual level across
  heterogeneous credit-information environments is unadjudicated with a demand-side population survey.
